  <h3 style="color: #4A148C;">Thematic Overview</h3>

  <p style="color: #1A0033;">
    This digital collection comprises 100 digitized black-and-white photographic negatives documenting the 1975 “Speak-Out! Charla! Bate-Papo! Contemporary Art and Literature in Latin America” symposium and its accompanying exhibition, “Plural: Artists of Latin America,” at the Blanton Museum of Art (formerly the University of Texas Art Museum) at the University of Texas at Austin. The Blanton Museum of Art holds the original photographs in its Speak Out! Symposium Collection, part of its Latin American Art Collection. Each image in our digital collection has item-level metadata, including a title, a description, and LCNAF-authorized names. The gallery and reception photographers are unidentified, and many attendees and artworks are unnamed. We described these without guessing, so gaps in the historical record stay visible.</p>
	<p style="color: #1A0033;"> Latin American artists, writers, and communities are often overlooked in the fine art industry, and this collection brings their voices forward. This collection features Latin American critics, writers, and artists discussing their own work and the questions facing it, alongside the artworks on view and the people who gather around them. For the UT Austin and Austin community at large, it documents a significant cultural event at the campus museum; a record that matters all the more as UT Austin moves to eliminate its Mexican American and Latina/o Studies major. For the humanities more broadly, users can see literature, art criticism, and artistic practices side by side, which supports teaching and research in Latin American art history and criticism. </p>
	<p style="color: #1A0033;"> Existing resources document similar themes primarily through texts, such as the ICAA’s digital archives of primary sources and critical writings by Latin American artists and critics, or through objects, such as the Blanton’s online listing for Plural. This digital collection adds photographs of the symposium’s panels, gallery installations, and the reception, showcasing a visual representation of the debate and people behind those texts and objects.</p>
  <p style="color: #1A0033;"> Our exhibition is organized into three themes. Discussion (22 photographs by Julia Dawson) shows the symposium's literature and art-criticism panels and their audiences. Exhibition (56 photographs) shows the Plural galleries and the artworks on view. Connection (22 photographs) shows critics, artists, and panel speakers conversing at the exhibition reception. Together they trace one question: whether Latin American cultural expression should follow local or imported models, from debate to artistic practice, to the people who carried the conversation forward. </p>
